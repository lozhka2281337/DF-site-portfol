Fortress field notes

How a Dwarf Fortress is built

In Dwarf Fortress, the fortress is not just decoration: it is a complex engineering system. A well-designed fortress balances logistics, defense, the psychological comfort of its residents, and complete production chains.

The architecture is always three-dimensional, built across Z-levels. Building one enormous flat layer is a logistical disaster because dwarves move vertically through stairs much faster than they travel through long horizontal corridors. A classic, efficient fortress is divided into several key zones, arranged from the surface downwards.

1. Surface and entrance zone

This is the face of the fortress, its first defensive line, and its point of contact with the outside world.

  • Trade Depot: a large room where human, elven, and dwarven caravans arrive. It needs wide, safe access from the surface.
  • Airlock and trap systems: a winding corridor, often 20–30 tiles long, filled with mechanical, cage, or weapon traps.
  • Drawbridges: retractable bridges connected to levers. During a siege or goblin raid, they rise and seal the fortress.
  • Entrance barracks: training rooms for military dwarves, ready to intercept anything that breaks through.
  • Pastures and farms: animal pens and outdoor crop plots, often surrounded by a high wall to form a safe inner yard.

2. Upper underground levels: logistics and agriculture

These levels are dug through the first layers of soil, clay, or sand directly below the surface. The soft ground is ideal for farming.

  • Underground farms: plots for growing Plump Helmets, the foundation of dwarven food and brewing.
  • Underground pastures: habitats for cave animals.
  • Food block: kitchens, stills, and large food stockpiles placed directly below the farms for fast processing.

3. Middle levels: public life

This is where dwarves spend their free time. It should be made as beautiful as possible, with smoothed and engraved stone walls to improve the population's mood.

  • Central stairwell: a vertical 2×2 or 3×3 shaft running through the fortress and serving as its main transport hub.
  • Tavern and meeting hall: the social center where dwarves drink, dance, talk, and welcome mercenaries, poets, and visitors. Bedrooms are often nearby.
  • Dining hall: a large, richly decorated room filled with tables and chairs.
  • Hospital: beds, chests for bandages, plaster, and sutures, with a reliable source of clean water nearby.
  • Temples and libraries: spaces that satisfy the spiritual and intellectual needs of citizens.

4. Residential quarters

Residential areas are usually kept away from noisy production zones or placed on separate Z-levels above or below the tavern.

  • Dormitory: a shared bedroom with many beds for newly arrived migrants.
  • Private rooms: compact 1×3 to 3×3 bedrooms with a bed, chest, and cabinet.
  • Nobles' quarters: luxurious apartments for a mayor, baron, or king, including a throne room, private dining room, and tomb. If their demands are not met, nobles begin issuing unreasonable mandates.

5. Lower levels: industrial sector

This is the industrial core, dug deep into solid stone.

  • Craft workshops: carpenters, masons, leatherworkers, clothiers, and jewelers.
  • Magma smelters and forges: placed near natural magma on deep levels so experienced players can smelt ore without spending charcoal.
  • Raw-material stockpiles: large spaces around workshops filled with stone, wood, ore, and bars. Quantum stockpiles and carefully tuned Give/Take chains are often used to reduce walking distance.

6. Deep levels: mines and crypts

  • Crypts and tombs: rooms with coffins. Dwarves respect their ancestors, and an improperly buried companion may return as a ghost and drive the living insane.
  • Mining shafts: long branching corridors made to search for iron, gold, platinum, gems, and other valuable resources.
  • Cavern access: miners eventually break into enormous underground biomes with rivers, giant spiders, and Forgotten Beasts, but also underground trees and rare resources.

Golden rules of fortress anatomy

  1. Stockpile above the workshop: raw materials should be directly above the workshop, with finished goods below it. A dwarf takes one stair step instead of walking twenty corridor tiles.
  2. Water safety: every river-fed water system needs floodgates and diagonal sections to reduce pressure. One misplaced tile can flood the entire fortress.
  3. Break-in prevention: cavern borders and entrances need controllable grates, bridges, or fortifications so flying and amphibious creatures cannot surprise the fortress.

How the map is generated

Every regeneration creates a fresh 80 × 80 fortress from a random layout.

  1. Split the 80 × 80 rock into balanced BSP sectors.
  2. Carve 5–8 medium-sized rectangular rooms inside the sectors.
  3. Connect every room to the central stairwell with corridors.
  4. Add fan-shaped shafts, then soften edges with cellular automata.
